Bitcoin for coffee shops

What if a faster payment rail helped your coffee shop keep more of every sale?

Coffee shops run on repeat customers, small tickets and lots of transactions. That makes payment friction and per-transaction economics worth examining closely.

Explore Square for coffee shopsCompare payment costs
Why it matters

Bitcoin has to make the business better.

When average tickets are small, even modest payment friction gets repeated hundreds of times. Bitcoin only deserves counter space if it works quickly, fits the Square workflow and makes sense across real transaction volume.

Keep more of each sale

Compare the cost of accepting Bitcoin with the payment methods you already use. The goal is better unit economics, not novelty.

Reduce payment friction

Use Bitcoin inside a familiar Square commerce workflow instead of adding a disconnected crypto checkout system.

Get paid efficiently

Evaluate settlement speed and operating cash flow alongside processing cost, customer experience and reporting.

Add customer choice

Give Bitcoin customers another way to pay without requiring every customer—or every sale—to use Bitcoin.

For this business

What the Bitcoin case looks like here.

Lots of transactions

Small differences in payment cost can compound across a high daily transaction count. Model the effect across a month or a year.

Speed at the counter

The payment experience has to be simple enough for a morning rush. Any extra step has to justify itself operationally.

Repeat customers

A regular who prefers Bitcoin can use it repeatedly, making adoption more meaningful than a one-time novelty purchase.

Start practical

You do not need to rebuild your business around Bitcoin.

Start with a few measurable questions: what does it cost you to get paid today, how many customers might actually use Bitcoin, how does the checkout fit your operation, and what happens if adoption grows from a small percentage of sales?
