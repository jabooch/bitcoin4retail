Bitcoin for retail stores

Give Bitcoin a job in your retail operation.

For a retail store, Bitcoin should be evaluated like any other payment rail: by cost, checkout fit, customer demand, settlement and the effect on your operating workflow.

Explore Square for retailCompare payment costs
Why it matters

Bitcoin has to make the business better.

Retailers already manage inventory, staffing, payments and customer experience. A new payment method only makes sense if it integrates cleanly and has a measurable reason to exist.

Keep more of each sale

Compare the cost of accepting Bitcoin with the payment methods you already use. The goal is better unit economics, not novelty.

Reduce payment friction

Use Bitcoin inside a familiar Square commerce workflow instead of adding a disconnected crypto checkout system.

Get paid efficiently

Evaluate settlement speed and operating cash flow alongside processing cost, customer experience and reporting.

Add customer choice

Give Bitcoin customers another way to pay without requiring every customer—or every sale—to use Bitcoin.

For this business

What the Bitcoin case looks like here.

Variable basket sizes

Compare Bitcoin economics across both everyday purchases and higher-ticket transactions instead of relying on a single example.

Existing Square workflow

The strongest case is operational simplicity: offering Bitcoin without building an entirely separate commerce stack.

Customer acquisition

For stores serving Bitcoin-aware customers, payment choice can become part of the reason someone visits or returns.

Start practical

You do not need to rebuild your business around Bitcoin.

Start with a few measurable questions: what does it cost you to get paid today, how many customers might actually use Bitcoin, how does the checkout fit your operation, and what happens if adoption grows from a small percentage of sales?
