How we compare Bitcoin payment products.

A consistent merchant-first framework keeps commercial relationships from becoming the recommendation system.

Merchant-first criteria

We evaluate products on the business outcomes and constraints a merchant can actually observe: processing cost, conversion and payout costs, settlement options, Lightning support, checkout experience, hardware requirements, POS or ecommerce compatibility, reporting, geographic availability and implementation effort.

Primary sources first

Pricing and product claims are checked against provider documentation whenever possible. Tax and regulatory topics are treated cautiously and point readers toward qualified professional advice where appropriate.

We compare the whole cost

A headline processing percentage can be misleading by itself. Where relevant, we also identify conversion, payout, withdrawal, network, hardware and operational costs.

No pay-to-win ranking

Affiliate payout does not determine a provider's factual treatment or comparison position. A provider with no affiliate relationship can still be included when it is relevant to the merchant decision.

Material limitations belong in the review

Eligibility rules, geography, settlement restrictions, refund differences, transaction limits and implementation tradeoffs are part of the product—not footnotes to hide.

Updates

Provider pages show review dates where pricing and product details are material. Payment products change quickly, so merchants should verify current terms with the provider before making a business decision.
